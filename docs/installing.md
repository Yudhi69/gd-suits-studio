# Installing GD Suits Studio

Written for the shop. Nothing here needs any technical knowledge — but there is
one warning screen on each operating system that looks alarming and is not, so
it is worth reading that part before you start.

---

## 1. Take the right file

There are three, and you need one. They all carry the version number, so
ignore that part and look at the rest of the name.

| Your machine | The file |
| --- | --- |
| MacBook with an Apple chip (M1, M2, M3, M4) | the `.dmg` with **arm64** in its name |
| MacBook with an Intel chip | the other `.dmg` — no `arm64` |
| Windows | the one ending in **`.exe`** |

If you are not sure which Mac you have: click the Apple menu in the top left,
then **About This Mac**. It says *Chip* (an Apple one) or *Processor* (Intel).

Taking the wrong Mac file is not harmful. It simply will not open.

**Which version of macOS you need.** macOS 10.15 Catalina or anything newer.
The same About This Mac window shows yours, under the name - for example
"macOS Catalina, Version 10.15.8". On Windows, Windows 10 or 11.

---

## 2. Install it

### On a Mac

1. Double-click the `.dmg` file.
2. A window opens with the GD Suits Studio icon and a shortcut to
   **Applications**. Drag the icon onto Applications.
3. Open Applications and find GD Suits Studio.
4. **The first time only:** hold Control and click the icon, then choose
   **Open** from the menu that appears. The same warning appears, but this
   time it has an **Open** button. Click **Open**.

That Control-click matters. Double-clicking the first time gives you this
box, with no Open button at all:

> "GD Suits Studio" can't be opened because Apple cannot check it for
> malicious software. This software needs to be updated. Contact the
> developer for more information.

Nothing is wrong with the app, and you do not need a newer copy. That
wording is what macOS shows for any app Apple has not checked itself. Click
**Cancel**, not Move to Trash, and do the Control-click.

**If there is still no Open button**, use the Apple menu. Do this within an
hour of trying to open the app, because the button below only appears
for an hour after a blocked attempt.

- **macOS Catalina, Big Sur or Monterey** (10.15, 11 and 12): **System
  Preferences** → **Security & Privacy** → the **General** tab. Near the
  bottom, beside the message about GD Suits Studio, click **Open Anyway**.
  If it is greyed out, first click the padlock in the bottom left corner and
  type your Mac password.
- **macOS Ventura or newer** (13 and up): **System Settings** → **Privacy &
  Security**. Scroll down and click **Open Anyway** beside the message about
  GD Suits Studio.

Then click **Open** in the box that follows. On macOS 15 and newer the
Control-click no longer offers Open, so this is the way in from the start.

You only do this once. From then on it opens normally, from the dock like
anything else.

### On Windows

1. Double-click the `.exe` file.
2. A blue screen may say **"Windows protected your PC"**. Click **More info**,
   then **Run anyway**.
3. Choose where to install it, or accept what it suggests. It will offer to
   put a shortcut on the desktop; leave that ticked.

---

## Why the warnings appear

Apple and Microsoft each sell a certificate that lets them confirm who wrote a
program. GD Suits Studio does not have one yet — the Apple one is about
$99 a year and requires registering a business with them, and it has not been
worth doing until the app is in daily use.

The warning does not mean anything was found. It means nobody has paid to
vouch for it. You know where this came from, which is more than a certificate
would tell you.

When we buy one, the warnings disappear on their own — nothing about how you
install it changes.

---

## 3. First run

There is nothing to set up. The app opens on your orders, which will be empty
until you make one or until your existing records are brought across.

Everything is kept on your own machine. No client's name, photograph or
measurement leaves it, except when you press the button to render a preview,
which sends that one client's photograph and the design to the AI. That is the
only thing that ever goes out, and only when you ask for it.

Your work is saved as you type. There is no save button and nothing to lose by
closing the app.

---

## 4. Using it

The whole job is one page per step, along the top, and you can jump between
them in any order. A step with a tick has something in it. Nothing has to be
filled in before you move on - an order half-taken is saved exactly as it is.

**Making an order.** Orders, then **New consultation**. Put in the client's
name and you have an order; everything else can follow while he is standing
there.

The steps, in the order they usually happen:

| Step | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| **Client** | Name, telephone, email, date of birth, delivery address |
| **Order** | The people on the order and their suits, each suit's cloth, the informal quote, money in and out |
| **Base Garment** | 3-piece, 2-piece, single blazer, pants or waistcoat - and the shirt, tie or bow tie (with their colours), lapel chain, lapel pin, tie pin and cuff links |
| **Lining & Stitching** | Standard or custom lining, its colour and code, thread |
| **Jacket**, **Waistcoat**, **Pants** | Fit, lapels, buttons, pockets, vents - each with its own page |
| **Extras** | The detail of what was ticked on Base Garment: collar, cuffs, shirt buttons, a photo of the tie, the pocket square |
| **Photos & Cloth** | Photographs of the client, their shoes, the fabric and the lining |
| **Measurements** | Every measurement, in inches or centimetres |
| **Preview** | The AI render of the finished suit on him |
| **Fitting** | What happened at each fitting, and any alterations |
| **Summary** | The whole order, the formal quote, the order form, the client file |

**The price moves as you build.** Every choice that costs something adds to
the running total on screen, so the figure you quote is the figure the
choices add up to. On an order with several suits the total is all of them,
with the suit you are on shown beside it.

**Changing prices and wording.** Settings, then **Price list**. Type a new
price beside any option and every order that is not yet agreed follows it; an
agreed quote stays as it was until you re-quote it on the Summary page.
**Rename** beside an option changes the words shown for it - in the
consultation, on quotes and on the order form - without changing any order
that already chose it. **+ Add item** adds something new, such as belts, to a
page.

**A new copy starts with no prices.** The app comes with none of its own:
until a figure is typed in, a suit says *no price yet* and its quote cannot be
sent. A copy that already has prices keeps them through every update.

**Colours are typed.** Lining, tuxedo finish, buttons, shirt, tie and pocket
square colours are typed in words - "burgundy", "ivory" - because a colour
picked off a screen is not one you can promise.

**Several people on one order.** A wedding is one order. On the **Order**
page, add each person - the groom, the best man, his father - and each gets
their own suit, their own measurements and their own client file, waiting the
next time they come in. Each person folds away so the page does not fill up.
Each suit has its own fabric, code and supplier under **Order details**, and
**+ Add another suit** adds a second suit for the same client.

**The informal quote.** As soon as the base garment is chosen, **Informal
quote** (on the Order page, or at the bottom of every page) shows every line of
the price with its figure. Change any figure, add a line of your own - a
discount, a rush charge - or go back to the price list; the total and the
message follow as you type. It is emailed to the client with their renders
attached (tick the box off to leave them out), or sent by WhatsApp or copied
without the pictures. For a client under 18 it goes to the parent or provider.
When the client answers, mark it **Client confirmed** or **Asked for
changes**, make the changes, and send the formal quote from the Summary page -
which fixes the price at the figures you sent, as long as the order has not
changed since. Change the order and it goes back to the price list.

**The render.** On **Preview**, one press of *Render all four views* draws the
suit on the client from the front, the side, the back and at an angle. Click
between the four tabs to turn him round. If one view comes out wrong, *Just
the front* redoes that one without paying for the other three. **Refine this
render** changes the picture you are looking at while keeping his face and
pose - "wider lapels", "a shade darker".

The front is drawn first, and the other three are drawn to match it, so the
cloth is the same all the way round - a gold check stays as fine on the
trousers at an angle as it is on the jacket from the front. The **Photos &
Cloth** page shows the colours measured from the fabric photo and how much of
each the cloth has; that is what every view is told. If it lists a colour that
is not in the cloth - the table it was lying on - retake the photo closer.

**The shoes.** Put a photo of the client's own shoes on the **Photos & Cloth**
page and the render puts exactly that pair on him. Clear it and they are left
out.

**Alterations.** On the **Fitting** page, pick the garment and its
measurements are listed with the figure on file. Type the change against the
one that is changing - minus to take in, plus to let out - and logging it
updates the measurement. Cancelling or removing the alteration puts it back.

**The database.** **Database** in the sidebar is your workbook: Current
orders, Completed, Suit progress, Alterations and Extras, with the same
columns. Click any cell to change it - it changes the order itself. **Export
to Excel** saves the tab as a spreadsheet.

This is the one part that needs the internet and an AI key. Everything else
works with no signal at all.

**Sending things out.** On **Summary**: the formal quote email opens in your
own mail program, already written, for you to read and send. The order form and the
measurement form print or save as a PDF. **Client file** saves the whole
order - photographs, renders, specification - into a folder.

**Standing emails.** Settings, then **Emails**, writes a message once and
offers it when it falls due - a week before the event, say. It never sends
anything by itself: it tells you it is due and opens the draft for you.

## 5. Getting updates

The app looks for a new version each time you open it. If there is one, it
tells you once; after that a small flag in the corner reminds you, and
**Settings → Updates** has it whenever you are ready. You can look yourself
there at any time with **Check for updates**, and turn off the looking on
opening with the switch on the same page.

When there is a newer version it tells you what changed. Press **Update to**
the new version: it downloads it, checks it is genuine, puts it in place of the
copy you have and reopens the app on it - which then says **Updated to
version** and the new number. That is the whole of it - no file to find,
nothing to drag, no second button. If a render or an email is still going it
waits for that to finish first, so nothing you started is cut off.

On a Mac, the first time the new version opens it may ask for your password,
so it can read the keys you saved in Settings. Type it and choose **Always
Allow**.

A copy you opened straight from the installer disk cannot replace itself.
Drag it into Applications and open it from there first. The same goes for the
copy you have now if it is older than this way of updating: install the next
version by hand once, as you installed this one, and after that updates
install themselves.

**You always open the newest version.** On a Mac, if you open the app from
anywhere but Applications - the installer disk, Downloads - it offers to move
itself into Applications, replacing the older one there, so the Dock opens the
new one from then on. Press **Move to Applications**. If the older one is still
open it asks you to quit that first. If you open an older copy after a newer
one has been used, it opens the newer one instead, or tells you it is out of
date, with **Get the latest version** to take you to it. It never opens your
orders as an older version.

If an update cannot install itself it says why, and offers **Download**
instead, which saves it to your Downloads folder for you to install the same
way you installed this copy. If you are on the newest version it says **Up to
date** and there is nothing to do.

**Your orders, clients, photographs and measurements are not touched by an
update.** They live separately from the app itself. You can uninstall the app
entirely, reinstall it, and everything is still there.

The app only installs its own releases, only after checking each one is
exactly what was published, and never anything from anywhere else.

## 6. If something goes wrong

**It will not open on a Mac, and Control-click did not help.** Try to open it
once, then go to **Open Anyway**: System Preferences → Security & Privacy →
General on macOS Catalina, Big Sur and Monterey, or System Settings → Privacy &
Security on anything newer. Section 2 has the detail.

**It says it "needs to be updated" or "cannot check it for malicious
software".** That is the same first-time warning, not a problem with the
version you have. See section 2.

**It opens to a blank window.** Close it and open it again. If it happens
twice, that is a fault worth reporting — say what you were doing just before.

**You want to know where your records are kept.** Settings → About & data → **Open
data folder**. Copy that folder somewhere safe and you have a complete backup
of every order, photograph and measurement.

**You want to move to a new machine.** Install the app there, then copy that
data folder across, replacing the empty one. Everything comes with it.
